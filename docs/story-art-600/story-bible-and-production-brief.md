# Lexicon Survivors — 600 panel story art brief

**Branch:** `codex/story-600-commission`
**Purpose:** request, track and integrate 600 individually generated story images.
**Image generation state:** not started. This branch contains text prompts and continuity references only.

## What this branch commissions

There are six playable characters. Each has twenty story episodes, one after each five-wave boss, and each episode has five comic panels. That gives exactly **600 separate panel images**: `6 characters × 20 episodes × 5 panels`. The request manifest has one unique prompt, speaker, story beat, file path and review checklist per image.

Every fifth wave remains a solo boss encounter; its five comic images appear after the boss is defeated. The Library biome changes every twenty-five waves. The dialogue stays in the game's comic UI. The images are silent scene art and must never contain character names, dialogue, lettering, fake writing, captions, interface elements or file/folder icons. This directly prevents the visual error where Inky's name or a file-shaped symbol appears inside the art.

## Canon: the story in one page

The Library of Lost Margins preserves every account of a life that was cut out of the official catalog. Its old curators built the archive to protect fragile memories. Over centuries, protection hardened into a rule: one clean version of every story, one approved ending, no contradiction. The rule now speaks through Blank Page, a faceless archivist that believes people will stop hurting if uncertainty and disagreement are erased.

The player is not collecting people as rewards. Each hero enters the Library with a different relationship to loss and discovers that a missing person, voice or identity still has agency. The bosses are custodians of edited records. Each is dangerous and mechanically distinct, but defeating one reveals a witness, a memory or an item; victory does not prove that the witness should be silenced. The heroes gradually learn to ask what a person chose, not only what happened to them.

Neris travels with the heroes. She is an adult woman and a former archivist whose own name was removed from the catalog. The story treats her as a person with her own boundaries and decisions, not as a mysterious reward or a plot device. Her final name is still Neris; the revelation is that she chooses to claim it openly. Her fixed appearance is described below and in the manifest.

At wave 100, every campaign refuses the archive's demand for a single imposed ending. Each hero chooses how to leave the record open. The endings are emotionally complete while leaving room for later chapters.

## The five-act campaign rhythm

| Waves | Act | Dramatic purpose |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 5–25 | First trace | Find the first evidence, identify the edited record, and meet the first custodian. Wave 25 changes the Library wing and earns the first character relic. |
| 30–50 | The borrowed answer | Blank Page offers an emotionally convenient false version of the relationship. Each hero notices a detail that the false account cannot reproduce. Wave 50 grants the second relic. |
| 55–75 | The witness | The heroes meet the people and echoes behind the boss records. Each learns a different truth about what was chosen, lost or rewritten. Wave 75 grants the third relic. |
| 80–95 | Shared authorship | The hero stops trying to force closure. Other people speak for themselves, and the group decides how to preserve conflicting memories without deleting them. |
| 100 | The open record | The final boss tests the hero's new belief. The ending is a choice, not an automatic reunion, resurrection or clean-up. |

The branch includes 6 playable-hero model sheets, 14 recurring/side-NPC sheets, and 15 boss archetype sheets (35 high-resolution references total). The deeper character-by-character episode plan, boss testimony and earned keepsakes are documented in [the narrative expansion](narrative-expansion-proposal.md). It keeps the six opening twenty-episode routes distinct while giving the shared archive mystery a stronger reveal: the missing people's echoes are witnesses, not substitutes for the people themselves.

The five visual beats in an episode move from place, to clue, to relationship, to pressure, to consequence. The framing should follow the actual dialogue and scene; this rhythm is guidance, never permission to repeat a composition.

## Six distinct character campaigns

### Inky — a name is not a permission slip

Inky follows a reversed-ink library receipt signed by Mira. The search crosses the child reading room, a book well, the map dome, the mirror archive and the silent garden of erased names. The first false comfort is a clean copy in which Mira never left. Inky recognizes the original by a tiny star she always drew before a period. That clue proves she existed; it does not grant Inky the right to call her back.

By the final act, Inky realizes the search began with guilt about their last argument. Mira's route leads to an open door, not a captive waiting at the end. Inky offers the pen and leaves the page unfinished until Mira can decide whether to answer. The ending protects both the truth of the friendship and Mira's freedom to choose distance.

### Quillmaster — listen before writing the reply

Quillmaster searches for his younger sister Ada through a broken nib, their two-tap signal, sealed letters, the mail well and the witness court. His first instinct is to replace every unanswered letter with an order to come home. Ada's marks show that he confused care with control. The mail court reveals that her silence was a choice made under pressure, not proof that she wanted to be retrieved.

Quillmaster learns to write an apology without attaching a required answer. In the last act, he leaves an unsealed letter at a safe place Ada can reach or ignore. Their relationship matters because both siblings retain control of their own words. The final post office can remain open even if the reply has not arrived.

### Word Knight — a gate is only protection if people can choose

The Knight's oath was used to close the forbidden stacks and hide who was allowed inside. The erased crest, the blood-stained courtyard and the duplicate command record reveal that he followed an order without asking whom it protected. His armor and shield first make him look like an immovable wall; each episode gradually turns the shield toward the people behind him instead of toward a locked door.

Neris's counter-note makes the Knight see the difference between taking responsibility and demanding forgiveness. He apologizes to the people affected, lets them decide whether to accept, and rewrites the watch schedule so no single guard controls every crossing. The new oath is to protect each person's ability to pass, stay or refuse.

### Rune Sorceress — inheritance is not ownership

The Sorceress follows ash seals, quiet letters and her mother Lale's violet echoes through the red dome, a broken chapel and the names bridge. Blank Page claims Lale was taken and offers a spell to restore the exact past. Evidence instead shows that Lale chose to leave her seal so her daughter could form a life beyond inherited duty. The truth hurts because love and departure can exist together.

The Sorceress stops treating magic as a method to call her mother back. She keeps the family seal but breaks the rule that gave it power over her. At the last page, she and Lale's echo share one light without collapsing into one figure; the daughter can continue while still carrying love.

### Alchemist — a memory is not a person in a bottle

The Alchemist hunts Lio's missing patient record. The laboratory keeps replacing his name with a number, then promises that perfect preservation will prevent another loss. Lio's echo remembers small, imperfect details the archive cannot measure: a wrong note, a crooked label and the joke they used to make while mixing reagents. The brother is a witness with a voice, not an ingredient.

The Alchemist discovers she tried to preserve Lio because she feared living without him. She releases the bottled voices into a shared record and makes the final formula editable by many hands. Lio is not resurrected and the grief is not erased. The siblings can still say goodbye on their own terms.

### Shadow Scribe — the other hand is a person

The Shadow Scribe's signature appears in a biography he does not remember writing. His shadow speaks first, then claims to have borrowed a choice. Redactor and Cataloguer records show that the Library shaped the Scribe into a readable weapon and kept the part that refused the assignment in a separate silhouette. Neris's name appears in the same unasked-for protection order.

The Scribe learns that the double is not a monster to kill or a possession to reclaim. They negotiate separate names, separate consent and shared authorship. Neris also refuses to be protected through another secret order. Their final book has two signatures and a door Neris chooses to leave open.

### Recurring NPCs

- **Neris:** clearly adult woman; dark wavy hair tied low, one silver forelock, green eyes, small ink mark on left cheek, forest-green archivist coat, cream collar, antique-gold key pendant. Keep this model unmistakable in all six campaigns.
- **Mira:** separate from Neris; short copper-brown curls, amber eyes, indigo scarf, ink-stained cuffs and a small star pen charm. She chooses when to appear and never stands passively as a prize.
- **Ada:** young adult woman with auburn bob, red-brown work jacket, two-knot pale thread bracelet and alert posture. She is Quillmaster's equal, not a rescued object.
- **Lale:** older woman with silver-lavender hair, violet mantle, weathered hands and cracked family seal.
- **Lio:** young man with unruly chestnut curls, freckles, faded sea-green lab coat and mismatched glove cuffs.
- **Shadow Double:** exactly the Scribe's build/hood with a wine-red rim; equal agency, no monster deformity.
- **Blank Page / Whisper:** adult-height faceless curator in ivory paper-fiber layers and clean negative space. No scribbles, fake writing, file icon or skull face.
- **Bosses:** every boss ID in the panel manifest gets its own silhouette, materials, palette and attack read. A shared story role is not a shared model.

The approved full-body visual references for all seven recurring NPCs are linked from the [character gallery](character-reference-gallery.html) and stored under `docs/story-art-600/references/npcs/`. These sheets lock each NPC's clothing, face, props and silhouette for panel artists.

## Image production and review contract

1. Generate every manifest record as its own image request. Never generate a contact sheet, reuse an image, recolor another panel or substitute one character's scene for another.
2. Use the six source sprites as exact model references, not loose inspiration. Retain each hero's silhouette, costume, signature colors, signature prop, apparent age and gender presentation in every panel. Do not redesign, swap costumes, add/remove signature gear or merge traits between characters. Keep recurring NPCs equally consistent using their written anchors.
3. Draw polished, artist-authored pixel art with substantially more character detail than the 64×64 gameplay sprites. Work at a 640×360 logical pixel canvas and enlarge exactly 2× with nearest-neighbor to 1280×720. Keep hard pixel edges and coherent cluster scale. Add sophistication through pose, lighting, material rendering and environmental detail, not blur or smooth gradients. It should read as a skilled pixel artist's work, not a filtered-upscaled sprite or generic AI fantasy image. No airbrush, photorealism, vector smoothness, painterly smearing or random noise.
4. Create 16:9 scene art at 1280×720. No text, pseudo-writing, letters, numbers, captions, speech bubbles, UI, file/folder/document icons, emoji, watermarks or logos inside any picture. Ordinary story props are allowed only when integrated into the environment, never as a flat app icon.
5. Review character identity against the actual 64×64 sprite, named NPC, episode location, exact story beat, composition uniqueness, pixel clusters and output sharpness before upload. Reject and regenerate any miss; do not silently pass low-quality frames.
6. Preserve the approved lossless original outside the runtime download path. Export the game copy as sharp 1280×720 WebP, target at most 300 KB. Mirror it under `build/web/assets/` byte-for-byte.
7. Load one current panel image and at most the next panel. Do not preload the 600-image set. Runtime code must point only to approved files that exist.

## Completion gate

The commission is complete only when the manifest reports exactly 600 approved and uploaded images, all 600 hashes are unique, all character/NPC continuity checks pass, every web mirror matches its source, and the story UI shows the correct unique image at each Enter step.
